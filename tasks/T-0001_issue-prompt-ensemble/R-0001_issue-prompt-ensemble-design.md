# Vary the route, fix the target, select the medoid

**Bottom line.** For a task whose output should *converge* on one canonical, stance-neutral issue question, a useful prompt ensemble varies the **route** to the answer and holds the **estimand** fixed. The routes worth varying are disjoint few-shot exemplar sets, a decompose-then-render chain, a claim-then-neutralize chain, and context-aware vs context-blind input. The estimand is the target granularity, the output form and the neutrality rule, and it should not change between members. Personas, "perspectives" in the sense of disciplinary lenses, and abstraction level all change the estimand. The literature says they add variance, not accuracy, and for this pipeline they would inject frame words or create parent/child issue pairs that land inside the cosine margin band. The aggregation the free-form literature actually supports is **selection, not averaging**. Take the medoid (MBR) string among the members' outputs, embed only that string, and use the members' dispersion as a per-item confidence signal. has_issue gets a plain majority vote with an explicit "uncertain" outcome. EXP-007's numbers fit this picture. A median 1-cos of about 0.10 implies an average within-item cosine near 0.9. That is the redundant-view regime, where MetaEOL found paraphrase ensembles *worse* than one prompt and where averaging buys little, which matches the split-half ARI rising only from 0.71 to 0.75. The recommendation is 4 members (one more optional), medoid aggregation, and an offline check that sets noise floors first and guards against "stable but generic" collapse with reverse-retrieval specificity. A roughly 300-pair human same-issue set breaks ties. Evidence is thin at exactly the point that matters most. No published study ensembles prompts for a normalization output that is then embedded and clustered, so most transfer below is by analogy and is labelled that way.

**Verification legend (kept from the research notes).** **[V]** means a researcher checked the claim against the primary full text or HTML. **[A]** means it was checked only at abstract, search-snippet or secondary-summary level. **[M]** means it was cited from memory and not re-verified, so treat any specific number as needing a spot-check. Evidence levels for the design choices are **Direct** (shown on this kind of task), **Analogous** (shown on a neighbouring task or output type) and **Inference** (reasoned from the evidence, not tested). All examples are synthetic.

**How I read the has_issue figure.** The report reads "33-38 of 38 blocks" as *each prompt marks between 33 and 38 of the 38 blocks has_issue = true*. On that reading, disagreement is confined to a handful of borderline blocks and the flag sits near ceiling.

## 1. Members should differ in how they reach the issue, not in what issue they aim at

### Generation ensembles: the gains come from route diversity in a fixed answer space

Nearly every published prompt ensemble was built for tasks with a discrete answer, and the members that paid off changed the *route* while the answer space stayed fixed. **Self-consistency** samples reasoning paths from one prompt and majority-votes the answers, with large reasoning gains on PaLM-540B (GSM8K +17.9 points) [M] ([Wang et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171)). **DiVeRSe** varies the *few-shot exemplar set* and aggregates with a verifier [M] ([Li et al., ACL 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02336)). **Ask Me Anything** recasts inputs into open QA formats and aggregates with a weak-supervision label model that estimates each prompt's accuracy and the dependencies between prompts, reported at +10.2% for GPT-J-6B [M] ([Arora et al., ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02441)). **MoRE** uses four specialised reasoning prompts and treats agreement between experts as an abstention signal [M] ([Si et al., Findings EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14628)). **Mixture-of-Prompts** *routes* each input to one expert prompt rather than averaging [M] ([Wang et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00256)).

The most direct study of *which* prompt set to ensemble is **DIPPER**. It picks prompts greedily by "fidelity-adjusted semantic volume" (log-det diversity of prompt embeddings plus α times dev accuracy). Qwen3-1.7B on MATH went from 47.38 (same-prompt self-ensemble) to 51.57 at n=3. Diversity-only selection admitted "irrelevant or poor performing prompts", and the selection metric's correlation with accuracy rose and then fell as the fidelity weight grew. All runs used temperature 0.7, never 0 [V] ([Lau et al., EMNLP 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15238v2)). Classical ensemble theory says the same thing more formally. In the ambiguity decomposition, ensemble error = mean member error − mean ambiguity, so diversity helps only when it does not raise member error by more than it adds spread [M] ([Krogh & Vedelsby, NeurIPS 1994](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/1994/hash/b8c37e33defde51cf91e1e03e51657da-Abstract.html)).

This is where the team's instinct needs refining. "Meaningful dimensions" pay off only if every member is still estimating *the same* canonical issue question. A member that deliberately takes another perspective changes the target. In Krogh-Vedelsby terms that adds ambiguity *and* member error.

For free-form outputs, the only aggregation tools with evidence work by *selecting* a consensus candidate. **Universal Self-Consistency** asks the LLM to pick the most consistent of several free-form responses and matches standard SC on math [A] ([Chen et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17311)). **MBR decoding** picks the candidate with the highest expected similarity to the rest [M] ([Suzgun et al., Findings ACL 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07634)). **Multi-prompt MBR** decodes candidates from a *prompt bank* and selects by MBR, beating single-prompt MBR across tasks, models and metrics; the authors credit a more diverse and higher-quality candidate pool [A] ([Heineman et al., EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15343)). The closest output-type analogue, **GenQREnsemble**, ensembles paraphrased instructions for short keyword generation and reports up to 18% relative nDCG@10 gains [A] ([Dhole & Agichtein, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03746)). Its gain comes from taking the *union* for retrieval recall, though. The issue pipeline needs *convergence*, so that result does not transfer.

### Representation ensembles: diverse task framings help, paraphrases hurt

The embedding literature gives the cleanest support for the team's suspicion that paraphrase variants are not a real ensemble. **MetaEOL** averages last-layer LLM embeddings of the *same input* under 8 prompts drawn from 4 meta-tasks (classification, sentiment, paraphrase identification, information extraction). It beat single-prompt PromptEOL by +2.8 to +6.7 STS points (LLaMA2-7B: 75.96 vs 70.03). Mean (75.96) beat concatenation (74.99), which beat max-pooling (72.03). The ensemble beat its best single member by about 2.9 points even though one member (sentiment, 67.06) was below baseline [V] ([Lei et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18458v2)). The key negative control: **7 paraphrases of the PromptEOL prompt plus the original, averaged, scored 62.72, below the single prompt's 70.03** [V] ([same](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18458v2)). Gains came from *which* view was added (+3.58 for paraphrase identification, +0.56 for sentiment), not from how many prompts there were. The authors never ran an equal-size diverse-vs-paraphrase comparison, so "diversity is the cause" is their inference.

Query-expansion work splits into three families. One family averages into one vector: HyDE averages N hypothetical documents and argues the dense encoder acts as a lossy bottleneck that filters hallucinated detail [V] ([Gao et al., ACL 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10496)). A second concatenates the original with a generated view: LLM keyphrase expansion concatenated to the base embedding raised Bank77 clustering accuracy from 64.0 to 65.3 and Tweet from 57.5 to 62.0 [V] ([Viswanathan et al., TACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.00524v1)). The third keeps views separate and fuses scores or ranks: RAG-Fusion with reciprocal rank fusion [M] ([Rackauckas, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03367)), and multi-view retrieval with max-over-views scoring, which needed an explicit loss to keep the views from collapsing into the same vector [M] ([MVR, ACL 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08372)). Viswanathan et al. also observed that prompt framing decides *which facet* (topic vs intent) an expansion captures [V].

**What transfers.** MetaEOL averages *hidden states of one input* under different prompts. The team averages *embeddings of different generated strings*, which adds a generation-noise channel, so MetaEOL's gains are suggestive, not portable. What does port is the diagnostic. A median 1-cos of 0.10 means an average within-item cosine near 0.9 between the four EXP-007 outputs, the redundant-view regime. A second cheap ensemble axis sits on the *embedding side*: Qwen3-Embedding prepends an instruction to the query only [V] ([Qwen3-Embedding report, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05176v3)). Two or three task-framed instructions ("represent the policy decision being asked", "represent the affected actor and object") could be averaged MetaEOL-style over the *same* string. That costs no extra generation and has no direct evidence (Inference). Because anchors and comments live in one symmetric space, the same instruction has to be applied to both sides. Otherwise the instruction itself becomes a constant offset between anchors and comments, the analogue of PromptBERT's template bias [M] ([Jiang et al., EMNLP 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04337)).

### Temperature 0 makes prompt variance the main lever, and its disagreement is a signal

At temperature 0 the main controllable source of output variance is the prompt itself. DIPPER argues that same-prompt self-ensembles "still sample from the same distribution" [V] ([DIPPER](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15238v2)). Evaluation work treats paraphrase variance as noise to report as a range ([FormatSpread](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324); [Mizrahi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00595)). Uncertainty work treats it as *signal*: **SPUQ** perturbs prompts, scores agreement across outputs (e.g. by ROUGE-L), and reports about 50% lower expected calibration error [A] ([Gao et al., EACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.143)). In annotation, prompt disagreement tracked expert-defined ambiguity [A] ([Observational Equivalence, arXiv 2609.22133](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22133)), and LLM-LLM disagreement is used to locate fuzzy code boundaries [A] ([Disagreement as Data, arXiv 2601.12618](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12618)). Treat the ensemble's disagreement both ways: reduce it in the output by selection and exemplars, and keep it per item as a confidence score. Temperature 0 is also not perfectly deterministic. TopicGPT got ARI 0.92 between two identical runs at temperature 0 / top_p 0 [V] ([Pham et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01449v2)), and batch-dependent kernels are one documented cause [M] ([Thinking Machines Lab, 2025](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)). That floor has to be measured before any drift is attributed to wording.

## 2. Select the medoid string and recalibrate the margin, because averaging distorts cosine scale

### The options compared

### Why averaging moves the margin rule

For K unit vectors with mean m and average pairwise cosine c̄, the norm of the mean is ‖m‖² = 1/K + ((K−1)/K)·c̄, and the cosine of the renormalized mean with an anchor a is cos(m̂, a) = mean_i cos(vᵢ, a) / ‖m‖. This is a derivation, not a published result. At K = 4, the inflation factor 1/‖m‖ is **1.04 at c̄ = 0.9** (EXP-007's paraphrase regime), **1.09 at 0.8**, **1.14 at 0.7** and **1.27 at 0.5**. Two consequences follow. First, every top-1 and top-2 similarity of an item scales by the same item-specific factor, so the *margin* also inflates, and it inflates *more for exactly the items whose members disagree*. A fixed margin therefore admits the least certain items most easily. Second, the inflation grows as members become more diverse. Moving from paraphrase members to meaningful members makes mean-pooling *less* calibrated, not more. An unnormalized mean (equivalent to mean-of-cosines) has the opposite failure: it shrinks similarities toward zero for disagreeing items. Either way, a margin tuned on single strings is miscalibrated for averaged vectors. The medoid avoids the problem.

### Failure modes to test for

The generic-centroid risk is real and well grounded. In high-dimensional spaces, points near the data mean become hubs [M] ([Radovanović et al., JMLR 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/radovanovic10a.html)). Sentence-BERT spaces show hubness, and reducing it cut hubness by about 75% and error by about 9% [A] ([Nielsen & Hansen, NLDL 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18364.pdf)). Contextual embeddings are anisotropic, so random pairs already have high cosine [M] ([Ethayarajh, EMNLP 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00512)). Short issue strings share template tokens ("whether", "should"), which adds a common direction to every vector and compresses the cosine range (Inference; DS@GT's preamble leakage is the lexical analogue [V]). The predicted symptoms are a merged anchor group with a disproportionate in-degree of comment attachments, shrinking top-1 minus top-2 margins, and falling distinctiveness among anchors. Mitigations to ablate are mean-centering against the corpus mean, embedding the content span with the template stripped, and CSLS or per-anchor z-scoring when one group over-attracts comments (all Inference). On outliers: filtering bad generated expansions improved retrieval, so more views is not automatically better [M] ([Doc2Query--, ECIR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266)). A medoid or trimmed rule handles this; a plain mean does not.

## 3. Stability must be measured above a floor and paired with specificity, or the ensemble will reward generic issues

### Without labels: floors first, then prompt effects, then anti-collapse

Prompt-sensitivity work has moved to reporting distributions over variants: spread, mean, worst case and instance-level change. Instance-level change is ProSA's PromptSensiScore [V] ([ProSA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12405)), and the team's per-item 1-cos is already one. Variant counts in that literature run from about 10 to more than 100 ([SCORE](https://liner.com/review/score-systematic-consistency-and-robustness-evaluation-for-large-language-models) [A]; [PromptEval, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17202) [V], which estimates performance quantiles over 100 templates at about the cost of 2 single-prompt runs). Those counts are for *measurement*; a deployed ensemble can be much smaller. A factorial design attributes variance to prompt *components* rather than to whole prompts: 16 prompts per task shifted label distributions substantially [V] ([Atreja et al., ICWSM 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11980v1)). The cheapest and most specific instance of this approach is to use the members' own disagreement as the sensitivity estimate. A model-internal alternative that does not depend on embedding geometry is a POSIX-style log-likelihood check with the local model's log-probs: score string A under prompt B [V] ([POSIX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02185)).

Clustering-stability theory adds two cautions. Stability has to be normalized against the instability expected from random labellings with the same k [M] ([Lange et al., Neural Computation 2004](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976604773717621)). And stability is not validity. Consensus clustering produced apparently stable clusters on random unimodal data, which led to **PAC**, the share of item pairs with consensus between 0.1 and 0.9, calibrated against null data [V] ([Şenbabaoğlu et al., Sci. Rep. 2014](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/002642v2)). Stability is also governed by data symmetries rather than by the correct k [V] ([Ben-David et al., COLT 2006](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shai/sober.pdf)). At this scale, ARI is fragile. With 40 anchors in 15 groups, there are only about 25-40 within-group pairs out of 780, so a single anchor that moves shifts ARI visibly. Many small, unbalanced clusters favour AMI over ARI [M] ([Romano et al., JMLR 2016](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-627.html)). Element-centric similarity gives *per-anchor* scores that turn a global 0.6 into a list of the anchors that move [M] ([Gates et al., Sci. Rep. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-44892-y)). Confidence intervals must come from bootstrapping *anchors*, not pairs. Without that CI and a null, EXP-007's 0.71 → 0.75 cannot be distinguished from noise.

The decisive risk is **stable but generic**. A prompt that maps many blocks to "whether the policy should change" scores low 1-cos and high ARI. Across 37 replicated annotation tasks, about 31% of hypotheses reached wrong conclusions with state-of-the-art models, and F1 explained only about 7.7% of that risk [A] ([Baumann et al., arXiv 2509.08825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08825)). Two AI coders reached Cohen κ > 0.85 with a mean F1 of only 0.68 against humans [V] ([Kim & Mitchell, arXiv 2609.11109](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11109v1)). Measured sensitivity can itself be an artifact of the scoring method, and LLM-judge scoring reduced variance [A] ([Hua et al., EMNLP 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01790)). The embedding scorer can manufacture both spurious instability and spurious stability. There is no published specificity metric for question-form labels, so the guards have to be adapted (Inference):

- **reverse-retrieval MRR**: does an issue question retrieve its own source block among all roughly 170? This is a GoalEx-style description-as-classifier test [M] ([Wang et al., EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13749));
- **distinctiveness**: mean nearest-neighbour 1-cos among the 40 anchor strings, and the near-duplicate rate;
- **effective number of groups**: exp(H(group sizes));
- **attachment Gini**: how unevenly comments spread across anchor groups;
- **lexical genericity**: no content noun shared with the source.

Document coverage is a real gap for LLM topic methods. They scored high coherence but lower document coverage than baselines [V] ([Doi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00697)), which is why coverage sits alongside specificity.

### With a small labelled set: pairwise same-issue judgments, used to evaluate rather than constrain

Pairwise same-issue / different-issue labels evaluate both the scorer (ROC-AUC of raw cosine) and the partition (pairwise precision/recall/F1). Of the common extrinsic metrics, only B-cubed satisfies all four formal constraints, which matters if gold groups exist for a subset [M] ([Amigó et al., Inf. Retrieval 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10791-008-9066-8)). Using pairs to *constrain* clustering needs thousands of them: about 2,500 human pairs before PCKMeans helps, at about 3 pairs per minute [V] ([Viswanathan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.00524)). The small set should therefore calibrate the margin and k and choose between ensembles. It is not training data. Choosing ambiguous items by entropy beat random selection, and random triplets could even hurt [V] ([ClusterLLM, EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14871)). Pair sampling should accordingly be stratified toward near-margin and high-PAC pairs, keep a random stratum, and record inverse-probability weights. Binomial arithmetic gives a ±0.055 95% CI at n = 200 and p ≈ 0.8, so separating two ensembles that differ by about 0.05 F1 takes a few hundred pairs scored *paired* (McNemar on discordant pairs). Human agreement should reach Krippendorff's α ≥ 0.667 (tentative) or 0.80 (reliable) [M] ([Krippendorff](https://repository.upenn.edu/asc_papers/43/)). If it does not, the definition of "same issue" is underspecified and no prompt can be judged against it. Prompts treated as coders reached only α = 0.24-0.70 in one study [A] ([Reiss, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11085)).

### How papers choose the number and kind of members

Selection should weigh quality and diversity *jointly*. Selecting clusterings by both significantly beat using the full library, while quality alone or diversity alone was inconsistent [V] ([Fern & Lin, SAM 2008](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~xfern/ensem_select.pdf)). *Moderate* diversity beat maximal diversity in cluster ensembles [M] ([Hadjitodorov et al., Inf. Fusion 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.inffus.2005.01.008)). DIPPER encodes the same principle with a fidelity-weighted log-det objective [V]. Greedy forward selection with a held-out check is the standard procedure [M] ([Caruana et al., ICML 2004](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~caruana/ctp/ct.papers/caruana.icml04.icdm06long.pdf)). Returns diminish with *view correlation*, not count. In MetaEOL, one new meta-task added +3.58 while eight paraphrases subtracted 7.3 [V]. Self-consistency curves flatten toward the tens of samples [M]. For K = 3-5 candidates, scoring all subsets exhaustively is cheap. Take the smallest subset within one bootstrap SE of the best composite score, and run leave-one-member-out. A member whose removal *raises* specificity or F1 is harmful; one whose removal changes nothing is redundant.

## 4. Applied systems control label drift with structure (freeze, ground, select), not with prompt ensembles

No major applied system ensembles paraphrased prompts to stabilise generated labels. Each one stabilises the pipeline around the generator instead. **TopicGPT** generates topics, merges candidates with cosine ≥ 0.5 by asking the LLM about 5 pairs at a time, prunes low-frequency topics, then *freezes* the list and assigns with a supporting quote at temperature 0. Its ablation is the only published measurement of prompt perturbation's effect on the final partition. Against ground truth, Bills ARI went from 0.42 (default) to 0.39 with an out-of-domain prompt, 0.40 with shuffled order and 0.33 with 5 example topics. A repeated identical run agreed with the earlier one at ARI 0.92. Mistral-7B as the generator over-produced 1,418 overly specific topics [V] ([Pham et al., NAACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01449v2)). **LLooM** distils each document into 2-4 bullets of 5-8 words at temperature 0, closely analogous to the issue rewrite. It clusters the bullets with HDBSCAN, whose noise class means not every point is forced into a cluster, then writes one concept name *with explicit inclusion criteria* per cluster and scores documents against those criteria. It re-runs on outliers and on "generic" concepts matching 50% or more of the data. It reports 10 trials but no concept overlap across runs [V] ([Lam et al., CHI 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12259v1)). **TnT-LLM** runs 10 trials and *selects* the best taxonomy on a validation set, randomises option positions, then distils the labels into an embedding classifier that freezes assignment [V] ([Wan et al., arXiv 2403.12173](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12173)). **Clio** embeds LLM-written facet summaries, clusters with k-means and names clusters *after* clustering. It validated on 19,476 synthetic transcripts with planted topics and reached 94% reconstruction [V] ([Tamkin et al., arXiv 2412.13678](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13678)). BERTopic's LLM representation only *names* clusters, so its partition does not depend on prompt wording at all [M] ([BERTopic docs](https://maartengr.github.io/BERTopic/getting_started/representation/llm.html)).

Codebook and annotation work points the same way. **Codebook LLMs** found every tested model sensitive to category order, and models leaned on label-name semantics over definitions: generic or swapped labels hurt [V] ([Halterman & Keith, Political Analysis 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10747v2)). The *wording* of the generated issue question will therefore dominate any downstream LLM-based attachment, and shuffling the order of anchors in a comment-coding prompt is a ready-made sensitivity probe. Multi-agent coding converges lexically and semantically through discussion [A] ([BlackboxNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.blackboxnlp-1.12.pdf)). A higher share of resolved conflicts predicted *lower* accuracy [V] ([Kim & Mitchell](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11109v1)), so members should stay independent coders whose disagreement is logged, not debaters.

Argument mining supplies the canonicalisation machinery. KPA key points are stance-bearing claims inside a fixed topic and stance, so the team's issue question corresponds to KPA's *topic/motion* level [M] ([Bar-Haim et al., ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.371)). KPA makes strings matchable after generation: a single-sentence filter, a token cap, a no-leading-pronoun rule, quality scoring, and **redundancy removal by matcher score against higher-support candidates** [V via secondary description] ([Altemeyer et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00847v4)). The cluster-then-summarise approach, which reassigns outliers iteratively, outperformed earlier KPA methods [A] ([Li et al., ACL 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16000)). Claim normalization shows two more things. Gold normalizations of near-duplicate posts differ, which argues for evaluating assignment agreement rather than string agreement. And **retrieve-and-reuse** was a winning strategy: copy the existing normalization when cosine exceeds a threshold (0.60 for English) instead of generating a new one [V] ([DS@GT, CLEF 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17402v1)). Open-target stance detection predicts that viewer comments, whose targets are often implicit, will be the most variable [V] ([Akash et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00222v5)). Turning open target generation into *target selection* from a list (the TSE setup) is the most evidence-backed variance reducer for such texts. No KPA, claim-normalization or deliberation paper found here ensembles prompts or measures wording sensitivity of the generated strings. This is a genuine gap, and EXP-007 is itself new evidence.

## 5. Recommended design: four route-diverse members, medoid selection, and a floors-first offline check

### Fixed across all members (the estimand)

Every member uses the same rule block: one closed question of the form "whether [actor] should [action] [object]", 4-12 words, no stance verbs or evaluative adjectives, no frame or justification words, a self-contained subject (no pronouns), the same granularity definition, and the same has_issue definition with an explicit negative example. JSON fields are fixed and identical, and the string to embed is a dedicated field, so no preamble can leak into it. Fixing the template enforces format collapse on purpose ([Price of Format](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.836.pdf) [A]; [Project Debater](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03215-w) [M]). The embedding instruction for Qwen3-Embedding-4B is also identical, and applied to both anchors and comments.

### Ensemble members

Synthetic running example. The block reads: *"Interviewee says labs can't police themselves; regulators should require binding tests before release."*

|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Direct rewrite, exemplar set A** (2-3 synthetic canonical exemplars) | "whether governments should require pre-release AI safety tests" | Baseline route. Exemplars lower per-member sensitivity ([POSIX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02185) [V]; [ProSA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12405) [V]). 2-3 exemplars, not 5 ([TopicGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01449v2) [V]). | Analogous |
| M2 | **Direct rewrite, disjoint exemplar set B** (different topics, same template) | "whether regulators should mandate AI testing before release" | Exemplar-set diversity is the best-evidenced positive axis ([DiVeRSe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02336) [M]; [DENSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08780) [M]). | Analogous |
| M3 | **Decompose, then render**: extract {actor, action, object/aspect, is_contested} as slots, then fill the template | slots: governments / require / pre-release safety tests → same question as M1 | Aspect-first ([PAKPA](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.576) [A]) and target-first ([OTSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00222v5) [V]). Abstraction is used as an intermediate step ([step-back](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06117) [M]). The slots also give a lexical consistency check. | Analogous |
| M4 | **Claim, then neutralize**: state the block's strongest contested claim in one sentence, then convert it to the neutral question it answers | claim: "AI labs cannot be trusted to self-regulate safety" → "whether AI labs can be trusted to self-regulate safety" | Uses claim framing as a *route* (ClaimNorm-style [V]) while the output form stays fixed. Persona, disciplinary-perspective and abstraction-level members are deliberately excluded (Analogous evidence against: [Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10054) [A], [PSR4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05858) [A], [Cattan et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03853) [V], [Borchers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11198) [A]). If a coarse level is wanted, emit it as a *separate field* (a policy-area label in the style of the Comparative Agendas Project [M] ([CAP](https://www.comparativeagendas.net/))). Use it for blocking or roll-up, never as a vote. Paraphrase variants stay in the toolkit only as a *measurement* instrument for sensitivity.

For comments, a TSE-style mode is a stronger variance reducer than any ensemble (Analogous: [OTSD](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.00222v5) [V]; [DS@GT retrieve-and-reuse](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17402v1) [V]). The model sees the top-k anchor issue questions retrieved by raw-comment cosine and either *selects* one or writes "new issue". The candidate order is shuffled across members ([Halterman & Keith](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10747v2) [V]).

### Aggregation rule (pseudo-formulas)

For each item with member strings s₁..s_K and embeddings e₁..e_K, after dropping members whose JSON fails validation or whose has_issue is false:

- **Canonical string (medoid / MBR):** s* = argmax_i (1/(K−1)) Σ_{j≠i} cos(eᵢ, eⱼ). Embed s* alone and use that vector for anchor merging and comment attachment. (Analogous)
- **Outlier trim:** if K ≥ 4, first drop the member with the lowest mean cosine to the others when that mean falls below τ_out. Doc2Query-- style filtering against the source: also drop any member whose cosine to the source text is below τ_src. (Analogous)
- **Confidence:** c̄ = mean_{i<j} cos(eᵢ, eⱼ). Items with c̄ < τ_conf are flagged "ambiguous / multi-issue". Ambiguous video blocks are inspected for a split into two anchors (M4 vs M1 disagreement is the typical cause). Ambiguous comments go to "unattached / review" instead of being forced through the margin (Analogous: [SPUQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.143) [A]; [Disagreement as Data](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12618) [A]).
- **Attachment:** unchanged, Δ = cos(c*, G₁) − cos(c*, G₂) ≥ margin, with the margin re-tuned once on the human pairs. Because the medoid keeps single-string cosine scale, the existing calibration largely carries over (Inference).
- **has_issue:** majority vote. A tie, or a split with low c̄, becomes "uncertain" and is excluded from anchor formation pending review. No label model at K ≤ 5 (Analogous).
- **Comparison arms for the offline check, not defaults:** (a) normalized mean of member embeddings with a re-tuned margin; (b) mean-of-max (Chamfer) between an item's member set and the anchor group's member set; (c) USC-style *choose-one* by the LLM among the members' strings; (d) cluster-then-name: pool all K×N strings, cluster, and let the LLM write one canonical question per group ([LLooM](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12259v1) [V]; [Clio](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13678) [V]).

### Offline check on ~40 anchors and ~130 comment issues

**Step 0, freeze everything but one factor.** Fix the embedder, its instruction, normalization, the clustering algorithm, k = 15 (also try 12 and 20) and the margin. Build a small **planted-structure synthetic set** in Clio's style: about 20 synthetic note blocks written to known issues, including deliberate sibling and parent/child pairs, plus synthetic comments with known targets. This gives ground truth without real data.

**Step 1, noise floors.** Measure four floors: (a) the rerun floor, same member twice at temperature 0 with batch settings fixed; (b) the algorithm floor, one member, 50 runs over seeds and 80% anchor subsamples, giving pairwise ARI/AMI; (c) the random-partition null with the same group-size distribution; (d) the placebo-context floor already built in EXP-007. If floor (b) is near 0.7, most of the 0.58-0.71 cross-prompt ARI is geometry, not wording.

**Step 2, member behaviour.** Compute the K×K cross-member AMI matrix with an anchor-bootstrap CI. Per item, record c̄ (median, p90, max), has_issue entropy, element-centric per-anchor similarity and the 10 most unstable anchors. Build the co-association matrix across members and seeds and take PAC(0.1, 0.9) against a null (shuffled anchor-text assignment). The **downstream flip rate** is the share of the 130 comments whose attached anchor group, or unattached status, changes between members and between aggregation arms. That is the decision-relevant quantity.

**Step 3, anti-collapse.** For every member and every aggregation arm, compute reverse-retrieval MRR (issue string → own source among about 170), anchor distinctiveness and near-duplicate rate, effective number of groups exp(H), the largest group's share, the share of comments attached with Gini over anchors, and the has_issue positive rate. Any arm whose stability rises while MRR or distinctiveness falls is flagged as collapse. Also run a template-stripping ablation (embed only the {actor, action, object} span) and a question vs claim vs noun-phrase ablation on the synthetic set ("whether cities should ban cars downtown" / "banning cars downtown improves cities" / "downtown car bans").

**Step 4, size and composition.** Score all subsets of {M1..M5} of size 2-5 by the composite: stability above floor (b), with MRR non-inferior to the best single member and human F1 non-inferior. Pick the smallest subset within one anchor-bootstrap SE of the best, then run leave-one-member-out. Treat a member as redundant if its mean within-item cosine to the rest exceeds about 0.9; that is the EXP-007 paraphrase regime, and the threshold is Inference, to be calibrated.

**Step 5, about 300 human pairs.** Use roughly 120 near-margin or high-PAC pairs, 60 confident-same, 60 confident-different and 60 uniform-random pairs (anchor-anchor and comment-anchor). Store the sampling weights. Two annotators label everything and a third labels 100; require α ≥ 0.667. Split 70/30 grouped by anchor. The 70% calibrates the margin and k; the 30% reports weighted pairwise P/R/F1, cosine ROC-AUC and a McNemar test between the top two arms. At about 3 pairs per minute this is roughly 100 minutes per annotator.

**Step 6, report one row per arm.** Each row shows the floors, cross-member AMI [CI], PAC vs null, comment flip rate, MRR, effective number of groups and human F1 [CI]. State that at n ≈ 40, ARI/AMI differences below about one bootstrap SE (likely several hundredths) are not interpretable.

## Where the evidence is thin

The central gap is that **no published study ensembles prompts for a normalization or label-generation task whose outputs are then embedded and clustered**. Every quantified sensitivity study scores classification, MCQ or log-likelihood, not embedding drift of short strings. TopicGPT's frozen-list ablation is the nearest partition-level reference. There is **no direct comparison of mean vs medoid vs multi-vector aggregation for clustering short LLM labels**; the recommendations borrow from STS (MetaEOL), retrieval (HyDE, MVR, MUVERA) and MBR. **No instruction-ensemble study exists for instruction-tuned embedders**, Qwen3-Embedding included, and the circulating "1-5% drop without instruction" figure was not confirmed in the Qwen3 report. **No controlled study compares question vs claim vs noun-phrase phrasing** for embedding similarity. QANA's closed-question advantage is embedder-dependent and comes from GPT-4, not a local 27B model. **No study measures persona or perspective prompting on rewriting outputs** rather than accuracy, so the case against perspective members is inferred from accuracy studies plus the framing literature. DIPPER, the best evidence on selecting a prompt set, used temperature 0.7 and math reasoning only. MetaEOL never ran an equal-size diverse-vs-paraphrase control. The stability theory behind the protocol is mostly asymptotic. Its thresholds (PAC interval, about 300 pairs, the one-SE rule, the 0.9 redundancy cut, the strata sizes) are reasoned defaults with no validation on 40-item problems. Several applied-system details are tagged [A] or [M]: TnT-LLM's venue, Clio's appendix, k-LLMmeans' stability results, IPR's variant counts, and the Viswanathan et al. TACL venue. Some referenced works could not be located and were left out: HiCode, MindCoder, "Khosla et al. 2023", "Tweets2Stance".

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that EXP-007 did not show that prompt ensembles fail. It showed that **the four prompts were one estimator measured four times**, with a within-item cosine near 0.9, the same regime in which MetaEOL's eight paraphrases fell below a single prompt. The right fix is not more "perspective" as such, because perspective in the persona sense changes the target and pushes issue strings apart by frame or granularity, which a symmetric cosine-plus-margin rule cannot sort out. Diversity should enter through routes that converge on the same "whether..." question: different exemplars, a slot decomposition, a claim-to-question conversion, and context. The resulting disagreement is then informative, because it concentrates on blocks that genuinely contain two issues or depend on context.

The non-obvious consequence for aggregation is that **making members more diverse makes embedding-averaging less safe**. The renormalized mean inflates margins most for exactly the items the members disagree on. That turns selection (medoid), plus disagreement as an abstention signal, from a stylistic preference into a calibration requirement. The applied literature's strongest stabilisers are structural: freezing the codebook, anchor-constrained comment coding, and naming after clustering. Treat the prompt ensemble as one component inside that structure, judged by downstream attachment stability *and* specificity above measured noise floors, not by string agreement.
